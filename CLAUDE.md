## Layout
- `spike/fetch_irfcl.py`: gold volumes, fetch + clean, standard library only. `--out DIR` (the workflow uses `data/`), `--log FILE` (one CSV row per run). `--reserves FILE` (default `data/reserves_usd.json`, the last committed copy; if missing, that one check is skipped and the run says so) feeds the total-reserves rule. `--reclean FILE` (added 2026-11-11) re-applies the cleaning to a written `gold_tonnes.json` without the network (values restored from `rejected`/`repaired`, written reason texts kept where the reason is the same kind, so the file matches what a fetch would write); use it after a rule change, then commit the data with the code. The gold request itself is unchanged.
- **Cleaning rules (`screen()`, 2026-11-11):** month by month against the last kept value: an exact x1,000 / x1,000,000 slip (within 1%) from a figure of 1 t or more is **repaired** (divided back, listed under `repaired`: `[month, reported, used, reason]`); else **dropped** (listed under `rejected`) when over 9,000 t, a x1,000 jump from a sub-1 t figure (that month only), or worth more than the holder's total reserves at the median price (`over_reserves()`: same month or up to 3 back for the total, up to 6 back for the price; skipped when the holder's own gold value implies $35/oz or more, the US's $42.22 book value), then one-month dropouts (more than 50.1% off two neighbours within 5%; the 0.1% margin keeps an exact half, Belarus 2005-01, from being decided by rounding). The rule runs on the committed reserves file, so a new month whose total isn't in it yet is checked on the next run.
- `spike/test_cleaning.py` (added 2026-11-11): synthetic cases for each rule (Brazil repaired, a tiny holder's x1,000 jump dropped for one month, the first month back in the right unit kept, Kosovo dropped, the US kept, a dropout, no reserves file) and a no-op check: re-cleaning the committed `gold_tonnes.json` must change nothing. `test.yml` runs it after the gauge test.

## What it is
A personal project (the owner is the only user) that tracks gold accumulation and selling by central banks and large institutions. No audience, no monetization, no need to differentiate from existing sites (WGC Goldhub, Trading Economics).

**Purpose (owner, 2026-11-08):** one input among several for the owner's investment decisions in general, not only gold: track big gold transactions and their trend. Working assumption: that makes it a slow macro gauge, so monthly data is the right speed and daily sources (ETF holdings, price) drop in priority. Still open: which decisions it feeds (gold or miners exposure, a view on the dollar and US Treasuries, general risk appetite). That decides whether anything beyond IRFCL is needed.

## Key framing
Central bank gold trades are private, so individual transactions can't be seen. The product tracks *reported holdings* and how they change, with a delay. A large share of central bank buying is never reported (WGC: over 1,000 tonnes a year in 2022–24, much of it not traced to any country; in Q3 2026 central banks added a net 321 t, and about 63% of that activity was not disclosed at country level).

## Data sources (how often they update)
| Source | What it shows | How often |
|---|---|---|
| IMF IRFCL (replaced International Financial Statistics, now retired) | Official gold reserves for each country | Monthly, 1–2 months late |
| Central banks' own releases (China's PBoC, Poland's NBP, Turkey's CBRT…) | Their own holdings | Monthly or weekly, sooner than the IMF |
| World Gold Council (Goldhub) | Compiled stats, plus estimates of unreported buying (used: GDT central bank totals, typed in by hand) | Monthly / quarterly |
| Gold ETF disclosures (GLD, IAU) | Tonnes held by each fund (used: GLD, browser only) | Daily |
| CFTC Commitments of Traders | Futures positions (used: weekly job + alerts) | Weekly |
| US Treasury TIC (Major Foreign Holders) | Foreign official holdings of US Treasuries (used: monthly job) | Monthly, ~6 weeks late |
| IMF COFER | Currency composition of world FX reserves (used: monthly job) | Quarterly, ~3 months late |
| COMEX and London (LBMA) vault data | Metal in exchange and London vaults | Daily / monthly |
| Customs data (Switzerland, UK, Hong Kong) | Physical shipments between countries (Swiss open data: imports by country only; exports not scriptable) | Monthly |
| SEC 13F | Funds' stakes in gold ETFs and miners | Quarterly, 45 days late |
| USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries (history: Our World in Data, USGS + BGS) | Gold mine production by country | Yearly (late January; newest year estimated), revised a few times |

## How the page counts a month
- A holder's change in month m = its value at m minus its previous available value, looking back up to 3 months (so a dropped error value doesn't hide the move around it).
- Each active holder (reported within 6 months of the newest month) gets an *activity* weight: the sum of its absolute monthly changes over its own last 12 reported months. Month m enters the totals once holders with 90% of total activity have reported it. Weighting by holdings instead would let the US (26% of all gold, never changes, reports last) delay every total by months; by activity it weighs nothing, and Turkey (big swings) weighs a lot.
- **Without Turkey** (`SWING`, 2026-11-11): the same window and completeness rule with Turkey's monthly changes taken out (`months.get(i).swing`, `m.sumSwing`, `rolling12[].x`, `headline().x`). Turkey carries the largest activity weight (33% of all reported moves over the past year) and its figure also moves with gold Turkish banks place with the central bank and domestic swaps (−129 t in Mar 2026). Without it the 12-month net never fell below +230 t in 2026; with it, +155 t in Mar 2026. Below +160 t without Turkey: Dec 2009 (5 months), Dec 2010 (3), Nov 2013 (10), Jul 2020 (8), Jun 2022 (7); with Turkey: Dec 2009 (5), Dec 2010 (3), May 2014 (1), Aug 2014 (1), May 2022 (6), Mar 2026 (1).
- Groups: advanced = IMF WEO advanced-economy list plus the ECB; everyone else emerging/developing (regions were dropped 2026-11-09). Every 12-month figure uses the 12 months to the last complete month; a holder's later filings show separately ("Since then"). Momentum: a purchase is a month up at least 0.1 t; "started again" = a purchase in the last 6 reported months after none in the 12 before; "stopped" = none in the last 6 after 3+ in the 12 before.
- Breadth: holders whose 12-month change is at least +1 t ("adding") or at most −1 t ("cutting"), at each complete month. Concentration: the top two buyers' share of gross 12-month buying (every holder with an increase, summed). Not drawn where gross buying is under 25 t. Aug 2026: 17 adding vs 4 cutting (15 / 4 a year earlier; peak 18 in Jun 2026); Poland + China 56%.
- Trend markers: two fixed (Sep 2019 CBGA expiry, Feb 2022 Bank of Russia reserves frozen) and two found in the data (Dec 2009, when the 12-month total turned positive for good; Nov 2022, China's first reported purchase in 38 months). The shaded band before Dec 2009 is the net-selling era.
- First run (data of 2026-11-08): totals complete through Aug 2026 (Sep 62% reported, because Turkey hadn't filed). +320 t net in the 12 months to Aug 2026, +308 t in the 12 months before; last 3 months +159 t, about +634 t a year, so "speeding up". Poland and China are 56% of gross reported buying.

## Architecture direction
- No server and no database. A scheduled GitHub Actions job fetches the data, commits JSON files, and rebuilds a static site (GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages).
- Git history records each revision of the data ("git scraping"). This matters because the IMF and central banks revise figures after publishing.
- The same scheduled job compares new data with the last commit and sends the alerts.
- Pick one unit (tonnes) and convert on arrival; 1 tonne ≈ 32,150.75 fine troy ounces.

## Mine production (findings 2026-11-08)
- World mine output: 3,280 t in 2024, 3,300 t in 2025 (USGS estimate), 3,100 t in 2015; it has moved little since 2011. The World Gold Council's mine figures run higher (it estimates more small-scale mining), and recycling adds roughly a quarter more supply; neither is on the page.
- Reported central bank net buying as a share of world mine output: 9% in 2025 (+302 t), 7.6% in 2024; range −23% (2005, net selling) to +15% (2019). With the WGC's estimate of all central bank buying (over 1,000 t a year in 2022–24) it would be about a third.
- Producers and their own central banks, 2025: Kazakhstan's central bank added 57 t, 44% of the country's 130 t (it has first refusal on local output); Brazil +43 t vs 80 t mined; China +27 t vs 380 t (7%); Russia −6 t vs 310 t. Uzbekistan (130 t) and Ghana (150 t) don't report to IRFCL.
- The page also shows the share of mine output in the Supply tile's summary (it was a Pace tile until the 2026-11-09 redesign) and a "Mined in {year}" tile in the holder in depth (only for holders mining at least 1 t).

## Gold share of reserves (findings 2026-11-08)
- **Reported gold values can't be used as they are.** The US reports its gold at the statutory $42.22/oz ($11,041 M for 261.5 M oz; its total reserves show as $252.7 bn); Saudi Arabia, Korea and Singapore use historical cost; Chile's value is ~44x market (probably a unit error on a tiny holding). The fetch script's US tripwire checks that $11,041 M figure.
- **The market price comes from the data itself:** the median US$/oz across holders with 1 t or more, for months with 10+ holders. It tracks the market (2000-12 $273, 2015-12 $1,062, 2024-12 $2,609, 2025-12 $4,313, 2026-02 $5,211 from 66 holders, 2026-07 $4,050). Every holder's gold is revalued at it, and the reported gold value inside total reserves is swapped for that market value. No second source needed.
- **The world line starts Jun 2015,** when China (the largest reserve holder) first appears; before that coverage of today's reserves is ~70% and adding China would show as a fake drop. It ends at the latest month with 90% of reserves reported (Jul 2026 on 2026-11-08). A holder missing a month inside the line keeps its last non-gold reserves and tonnage for up to 3 months (an Oct 2025 gap otherwise made a fake 4-point dip).
- Result: gold was 8.5% of reported reserves in Jul 2015, 21.2% in Jul 2025 and 24.8% in Jul 2026 (peak 29.2% in Feb 2026). The rise is mostly price: +23% in price vs +0.9% in reported tonnes over the last 12 months. Largest holders: Germany 82%, US 81%, China 9%, Poland 31% (its stated target is 30%).

## Dollar, unreported buying, price (findings 2026-11-08)
- **Treasuries.** Foreign official holdings of US Treasuries: $3.76 tn in Aug 2026 (40% of all foreign-held), peak $4.26 tn in Feb 2020, −$164 bn over 12 months, −$369 bn since Feb 2022 while reported central bank gold rose 1,314 t (about $115 bn at each month's implied price) over the same months. TIC counts Treasury bonds and notes at market value and bills at face value (TIC FAQ 10a: MFH bonds and notes are "estimated by taking the market value as of the most recent TIC SLT report"), so part of that fall is the 2022 drop in bond prices, not selling; the page says so and doesn't try to split it. The TIC data is by custodian country (Belgium, Luxembourg, the Cayman Islands stand in for others), so only the official total is used.
- **COFER.** The dollar was 56.7% of allocated FX reserves in 2026 Q2 (71.2% in 1999 Q1, −0.3 pts y/y); euro 20.6%, renminbi 2.1%. Gold is outside COFER, so the page shows its own gold share (24.8% of total reserves) beside it rather than in the same chart.
- **Unreported buying.** WGC estimate vs IMF-reported: 2026 Q2 +296 t vs +145 t (51% undisclosed); last four complete quarters +790 t vs +236 t (70%). That share is this page's comparison (estimate minus the IMF changes counted here), not a WGC figure, and Turkey's filings are −58.8 t of the reported +236 t; the caveat says both. Reported buying was negative in 2023 Q2 (−59.9 t) and 2026 Q1 (−70.2 t) and above the estimate in 2017 (+471 t vs +371 t): those show a dash, not a share. The Q3 2026 WGC figure (321 t) can't be compared until Sep 2026 enters the totals.
- **Price.** Monthly reported net buying vs the month's implied price change, Dec 2009–Aug 2026: correlation +0.02 (none); average +30 t in months the price fell over 3% vs +27 t when it rose over 3%. Seasonality 2010–2025: March strongest (+41 t average), August weakest (+18 t).
- **Calendar.** On 2026-11-08 Sep 2026 was 63% reported and Turkey alone would complete it; 14 of 79 active holders were 2+ months behind. Typical filing delay can only be measured as `changes.json` accumulates (2 filings seen so far).
- **Revisions.** Nothing to measure yet: 2 figures tracked since 2026-11-08, none revised. The section fills in by itself.

## Rule of thumb
Every data source is something that can break. Limit scope by number of sources, not features. Don't add a second source until the first one runs end to end without manual steps.
**Exception (owner, 2026-11-08):** the owner asked for every meaningful feature at once, so GLD and CFTC were added before the reliability test ended. They live only in the page (browser fetch, panel shows an error if a source is down) and never in the daily job. A broken third-party source can't fail the IMF pipeline or the reliability count. Mine production (owner asked, 2026-11-08) follows the same rule: USGS runs in its own monthly workflow, OWID only in the browser.

## Spike findings (2026-11-08, first live run)
- **Endpoint.** IFS and the old `dataservices.imf.org` API are gone. Gold is IRFCL line 56, indicator `IRFCLDT1_IRFCL56V_FTO`, monthly. Only SDMX 3.0 works: `https://api.imf.org/external/sdmx/3.0/data/dataflow/IMF.STA/IRFCL/+/*.IRFCLDT1_IRFCL56V_FTO.*.M` with `Accept: application/vnd.sdmx.data+csv;version=2.0.0`. No API key. One request returns every country (2.8 MB, under 1 s). The SDMX 2.1 URL answers 403 / 404.
- **Units.** `OBS_VALUE` is fine troy ounces. `SCALE=6` on every row is a display hint, not a multiplier (US = 261,499,000 oz = 8,133.5 t). The script checks the US figure on every run as a tripwire.
- **Sector.** Use `S1XS1311` (monetary authorities + central government) only; every country has it for every month. The UK's gold is held by the Treasury (`S1311`), so a central-bank-only series shows the UK at 0 t.
- **Coverage.** 87 holders plus the euro-area aggregate (`G163`, skip it), 1999-12 to 2026-10. Present: US, Germany, Italy, France, China, Russia, Switzerland, India, Japan, Turkey, Poland, Netherlands, ECB (`EZB`), Portugal, Kazakhstan, Saudi Arabia, UK. Missing: Uzbekistan, and 11 more of 20 t or more (see v1 coverage). Kosovo (`KOS`) appeared in Aug 2026 with an impossible 160 t and is dropped (86 holders since 2026-11-11).
- **Ragged reporting.** Countries lag differently: in Nov 2026 most are at September, the US at July, the UK and Netherlands at October. The leaderboard must use each country's own latest month and show which month that is. Some series are stale (Brazil once its errors are dropped, Canada since 2019, New Zealand since 2024).
- **Unit errors in national submissions.** Angola (whole series) and Brazil (x1000 from 2026-03) report more gold than anyone holds. Brazil's are exactly its February figure x1,000 (172,446.344 vs 172.446 t), so since 2026-11-11 they are rescaled and listed as repaired; Angola has no good month to rescale from and stays dropped. The script drops values above 9,000 t and x1,000 jumps it can't repair, and lists what it dropped and repaired. A "jump over 25%" rule was wrong: it flags real moves (Hungary x10 in Oct 2018; Norway and Canada selling to zero).
- **One-month dropouts.** Some submissions have a single month far off two neighbours that agree within 5%, e.g. Kazakhstan 2026-03 = 0 t between 347.6 t and 354.3 t, which would show as a fake 460 t sale and re-buy. The script drops these (15 values across 11 holders; all are zeros or spikes that snap back). Turkey's 2026-03 drop (822 → 694 → 732 t) doesn't snap back and is kept as real.
- **WGC.** Goldhub's country holdings table re-publishes the IMF figures, so it adds nothing as a second copy (only backfill for countries IRFCL lacks, such as Uzbekistan). Its quarterly Gold Demand Trends estimate of *total* central bank buying, including unreported purchases, is different: the only public measure of the undisclosed share. A candidate later source, quarterly.
- **Tooling.** Claude's sandboxes can't reach api.imf.org. Live runs happen on this machine or on GitHub's runners. `python spike/fetch_irfcl.py --raw spike/out/irfcl_gold_raw.csv` re-runs on the saved response without the network.
